Understanding the disconnect between Gen Z and the workplace

Published evidence suggests that younger employees' workplace experiences cannot be explained by motivation alone. Development, feedback, manager support, financial pressure and labour-market conditions all form part of the picture.

Employer perspective

Concerns reported when hiring recent graduates

Survey responses from 966 business leaders highlight the areas employers most often identify as challenges when working with recent graduates.

Lack of professionalism 46%

Source: Intelligent.com survey of 966 business leaders.

Interpretation

The issue is broader than motivation

Employer concerns and employee expectations point to a possible gap between how workplace behaviour is interpreted and how younger employees expect to be supported.

01

Employers report concerns about initiative, professionalism and communication.

02

Gen Z places high value on structured development, feedback and mentorship.

03

The useful question is therefore not only "Why are younger employees disengaged?" but also "What conditions are they entering?"

Career direction

Progression is becoming less linear

Career development does not necessarily mean rapid promotion. Younger employees may value growth in different forms.

44% Steady progression

Prefer gradual career development.

25% Rapid advancement

Prefer moving upward quickly.

21% Lateral movement

Open to sideways or backward moves.

Retention implication

A development strategy built only around promotion may overlook employees who define progress through new skills, exposure, mobility or mentorship.

From evidence to action

Moving beyond reporting

The purpose of analysis is not only to identify a pattern but to turn that pattern into a business question that can be tested.

01 Evidence Employers report motivation and communication concerns.

50% cited motivation or initiative, while 39% cited communication skills.

02 Related evidence Gen Z places high value on feedback and development.

94% value feedback and reviews, while 97% value mentorship and on-the-job learning.

03 Insight Some apparent disengagement may coexist with unclear expectations or insufficient support.

This does not remove employee accountability. It identifies an additional factor worth testing.

04 Business test Introduce structured 30, 60 and 90-day manager check-ins.

Compare retention, attendance, performance and engagement against the previous onboarding baseline.
