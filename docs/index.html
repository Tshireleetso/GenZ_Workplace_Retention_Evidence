Workplace retention analysis

Understanding the disconnect between Gen Z and the workplace

Published evidence points to a more complicated retention problem than motivation alone. Expectations, manager support, skills development, financial pressure and the wider labour market all influence how younger employees experience work.

Research basis: IOL Cape Argus, Statistics South Africa, Deloitte and Intelligent.com

Reading the evidence

What stands out

The evidence suggests that the disconnect is not explained by one factor.
Employers report concerns around motivation, professionalism and communication.
At the same time, younger workers place strong value on training, feedback and mentorship.
This creates a management question: are expectations, feedback and support being made clear early enough?
High youth unemployment also means these workplace issues exist within a difficult South African labour market.
Development expectations

What Gen Z values at work

Formal training 98%

Career direction

Career progression is not only about promotion

Deloitte's 2026 global Gen Z and Millennial survey shows that career progression is becoming less linear.

44% Prefer steady career progression
25% Prefer rapid advancement
21% Open to lateral or backward moves
A retention strategy focused only on promotion may miss what younger employees mean by career growth. Skills, mobility, mentorship and meaningful work may also matter.
